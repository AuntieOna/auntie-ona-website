Phase 1

Getting ready for sounds

Before children learn to read individual letters, they need to tune their ears in! Phase 1 is all about listening — environmental sounds, body percussion, rhythm and rhyme and oral blending. These activities lay the essential groundwork so that when letter sounds come along, little ones are truly ready to hear them.

Phase 2

First letter sounds

This is where the magic begins! Phase 2 introduces the first 19 letter sounds, taught in sets so children can start blending simple words almost straight away.

satp inmd gock ckeur hbfff llss

Phase 3

Digraphs & more sounds

Phase 3 builds on Phase 2 by introducing digraphs — two letters that make one sound, like sh, ch and th — alongside vowel digraphs and more complex sounds. By the end of Phase 3, children will know all 44 phonemes in the English language — a huge milestone!

jvwx yzzzqu chshthng aieeighoa ooarorur owoiearair ureer

Phase 4

Blending it all together

Phase 4 isn't about new sounds — it's about using them! Children practise blending longer words with consonant clusters like st, tr and bl and segmenting for spelling. These videos help make that important leap from knowing sounds to actually reading and writing words fluently.

Phase 5

Alternative spellings

Phase 5 is where reading really takes off! Children learn that the same sound can be spelled in different ways — for example, the /ai/ sound can be written as ai, ay or a-e. They also learn to choose the right spelling when writing. These videos help make sense of the wonderful (and sometimes tricky!) patterns in English.

ayouieea oyirueaw whphewoe aua-ee-ei-e o-eu-e
